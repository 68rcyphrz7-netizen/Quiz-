Question 1 of 60Easy
What is a pathogen?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):A pathogen is defined as a microorganism capable of causing disease in a host.
Question 2 of 60Easy
Which term describes the ability of a bacterial species to produce disease?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: B):Pathogenicity refers to the ability of a bacterial species to produce disease, whereas virulence is the degree of pathogenicity.
Question 3 of 60Easy
Virulence is the degree of pathogenicity and is genetically determined by genes carried on:
Scientific Rationale (Correct: B):Bacterial virulence factors are genetically encoded on chromosomes, plasmids, or bacteriophages.
Question 4 of 60Easy
Which pathogens are capable of causing disease in immunocompetent individuals?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: B):Primary (major) pathogens can cause disease even in healthy, immunocompetent individuals.
Question 5 of 60Easy
Opportunistic pathogens cause disease mainly in:
Scientific Rationale (Correct: B):Opportunistic pathogens take advantage of immunocompromised or damaged hosts to cause infection.
Question 6 of 60Easy
Which of the following is one of the three characteristics determining virulence factors?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Virulence factors are determined by three characteristics of pathogens: invasiveness, infectivity, and pathogenic potential (of which toxigenicity is a major aspect).
Question 7 of 60Easy
A major aspect of pathogenic potential is:
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Toxigenicity—the ability of a microbe to produce toxins—is a major aspect of pathogenic potential.
Question 8 of 60Easy
What are the three main portals of entry for microorganisms into the host?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Microorganisms enter the host primarily through mucous membranes, the skin, or the parenteral route.
Question 9 of 60Easy
Which mucous membrane site is described as the easiest and most frequently used entry site for microbes?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: B):The respiratory tract is the easiest and most frequently used portal of entry for microbes.
Question 10 of 60Easy
Which entry route deposits microbes directly into tissues beneath the skin or mucous membranes?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: B):The parenteral route bypasses surface barriers by depositing microbes directly into underlying tissues (e.g., injections, bites, cuts, wounds, surgery, punctures).
Question 11 of 60Easy
What is the initial event in pathogenesis?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Adhesion—the specific attachment of bacteria to body surfaces—is the initial event in pathogenesis.
Question 12 of 60Easy
Adhesins on the bacterial surface bind specifically to:
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Adhesion is a specific reaction between surface receptors on host cells and adhesive structures (adhesins) on bacteria.
Question 13 of 60Easy
What are adhesins chemically composed of?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Adhesins are made of protein and are antigenic in nature.
Question 14 of 60Easy
Invasiveness indicates the ability of a pathogen to:
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Invasiveness is the ability of a pathogen to spread within host tissues after establishing infection.
Question 15 of 60Easy
What does 'toxemia' mean?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Toxemia refers to the presence of toxins in the blood.
Question 16 of 60Easy
Which toxins are lipopolysaccharides (LPS) forming part of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: B):Endotoxins are lipopolysaccharides (LPS) integrated into the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria.
Question 17 of 60Easy
What are toxoids?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Toxoids are toxins inactivated or altered by heat or chemicals while retaining immunogenicity, used as vaccines (such as for diphtheria and tetanus).
Question 18 of 60Easy
Which bacterial enzyme destroys phagocytic white blood cells?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Leukocidin destroys phagocytic blood cells (leukocytes), such as those produced by Staphylococci and Streptococci.
Question 19 of 60Easy
Which bacterial enzyme destroys red blood cells?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Hemolysins lyse red blood cells (erythrocytes) and are produced by organisms such as Clostridium perfringens and streptococci.
Question 20 of 60Easy
Which structure on Streptococcus pneumoniae helps prevent phagocytes from effectively contacting the bacterium?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):A slippery mucoid capsule (such as in Streptococcus pneumoniae) helps bacteria evade phagocytosis by interfering with effective phagocyte contact.
Question 21 of 60Medium
After achieving entry into the host, what host defenses must a pathogen overcome to establish itself?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Once inside, pathogens face phagocytosis, low pH in the stomach and urogenital tract, and hydrolytic/proteolytic enzymes in saliva, stomach, and small intestine.
Question 22 of 60Medium
Microbes entering via the gastrointestinal tract must survive which hostile factors?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):GI pathogens ingested via water, food, contaminated fingers, or fomites must survive stomach HCl, enzymes, and bile.
Question 23 of 60Medium
Why is unbroken skin generally impenetrable by most microbes?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Intact skin blocks most microbes, but some enter through hair follicles and sweat glands, or certain fungi grow on skin using keratin-degrading enzymes.
Question 24 of 60Medium
What happens if a bacterial strain loses its adhesins?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Because attachment is essential to initiate infection, loss of adhesins often makes a strain avirulent.
Question 25 of 60Medium
Which pair of attachment examples is correctly matched with its site?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):E. coli fimbriae aid attachment to the urinary tract, while the glycocalyx of staphylococci and streptococci promotes strong adherence to heart valves.
Question 26 of 60Medium
How do highly invasive pathogens differ from less invasive ones?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Invasiveness reflects spread in host tissues: streptococcal septicemia after wound infection exemplifies spreading infection, whereas a staphylococcal abscess is localized.
Question 27 of 60Medium
How does Clostridium tetani cause serious or fatal disease despite lacking invasiveness?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Clostridium tetani remains localized at the site of entry and exerts systemic effects by releasing a potent neurotoxin.
Question 28 of 60Medium
Which statement accurately contrasts exotoxin and endotoxin production?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Exotoxins are soluble protein enzymes/toxins (mostly Gram-positive, though some Gram-negative also secrete exotoxins), whereas endotoxins are LPS (lipid A) in Gram-negative outer membranes liberated when bacteria die and lyse.
Question 29 of 60Medium
Which functional category of exotoxins targets the gastrointestinal tract lining?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Enterotoxins affect the lining of the gastrointestinal tract, whereas cytotoxins damage/kill host cells and neurotoxins interfere with nerve impulses.
Question 30 of 60Medium
Which toxins are listed as important exotoxins in the lecture?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):The lecture highlights diphtheria toxin, erythrogenic toxins, neurotoxin, and tetanus toxin among important exotoxins.
Question 31 of 60Medium
Which toxic component of Gram-negative endotoxin is specifically named?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):The toxic lipid portion of lipopolysaccharide (LPS) endotoxin is known as lipid A.
Question 32 of 60Medium
Why do different Gram-negative bacteria producing endotoxins cause similar clinical signs and symptoms?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Endotoxins from Gram-negative bacteria act via LPS/lipid A upon cell lysis, producing common systemic manifestations including fever (pyrogenic response), chills, weakness, general aches, blood clotting, and tissue death.
Question 33 of 60Medium
Why can sterilized medical equipment still pose a risk related to endotoxins?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Endotoxins are heat stable (unlike heat-labile exotoxins), so sterilized equipment may still contain residual endotoxins.
Question 34 of 60Medium
Which group of bacteria is explicitly listed as producing endotoxins?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):The lecture lists Salmonella, Pseudomonas spp., Neisseria spp., and Proteus spp. (as well as E. coli in the comparison table) among endotoxin-producing organisms.
Question 35 of 60Medium
Which comparison between Exotoxin and Endotoxin is correct according to the lecture table?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Exotoxins are heat labile and elicit a strong immune response, whereas endotoxins are heat stable and elicit a weak immune response (do not promote effective antibodies).
Question 36 of 60Medium
What is the function of the bacterial enzyme lecithinase?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Lecithinase breaks down lecithin in cell membranes, destroying the plasma membrane.
Question 37 of 60Medium
What is the role of bacterial protease in infection?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Protease is a tissue-degrading enzyme that breaks down proteins in host tissues during infection.
Question 38 of 60Medium
In Corynebacterium diphtheriae, what is required for exotoxin production?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Corynebacterium diphtheriae produces diphtheria exotoxin only when lysogenized with betaphage; eliminating the phage abolishes toxigenicity.
Question 39 of 60Medium
What happens if the betaphage is eliminated from Corynebacterium diphtheriae?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Because the toxin gene is carried by betaphage, eliminating the phage abolishes the bacterium's toxigenicity.
Question 40 of 60Medium
Which specialized surface protein on Streptococcus pyogenes helps evade host defenses?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Streptococcus pyogenes utilizes specialized surface structures such as M protein to evade host defense mechanisms.
Question 41 of 60Hard
A clinician isolates a bacterial strain that has a normal chromosome but has lost a conjugative plasmid and been cured of a prophage. Which virulence property is most directly at risk?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Virulence is genetically determined by genes carried on chromosomes, plasmids, and phages; losing plasmids or phages removes traits encoded on those mobile elements.
Question 42 of 60Hard
Two bacterial species enter a wound: Species A causes rapidly spreading bacteremia/septicemia, while Species B remains localized in a pus-filled pocket. How does the lecture classify this difference?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Invasiveness is the ability to spread in host tissues after establishing infection—yielding spreading/generalized lesions (e.g., streptococcal septicemia) versus localized lesions (e.g., staphylococcal abscess).
Question 43 of 60Hard
A patient develops severe clinical signs of fever, chills, weakness, general aches, and blood clotting after antibiotic treatment kills a large load of a Gram-negative rod. What is the primary mechanism?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Endotoxins are structural LPS (lipid A) components of the Gram-negative outer membrane that are liberated when bacteria die and break apart, causing fever, chills, weakness, aches, clotting, and tissue damage.
Question 44 of 60Hard
Why do vaccines against diphtheria and tetanus rely on toxoids rather than native active exotoxins?
Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Toxoids are toxins altered by heat or chemicals so they can be safely used as vaccines (e.g., for diphtheria and tetanus) while stimulating a protective immune response.
Question 45 of 60Hard

Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):The lecture contrasts exotoxins (strong immune response; soluble in body fluids and transported in blood or lymph) with endotoxins (weak immune response; do not promote effective antibody formation).
Question 46 of 60Hard

Scientific Rationale (Correct: A):Bacteria establish and persist by attaching via adhesins, spreading via invasiveness/enzymes (like protease, lecithinase, leukocidin, hemolysins), and evading defenses via capsules, M protein, or surviving/multiplying intracellularly in phagocytes.
